Expert Strategy

Subject-Wise Study Tips to Score High

Ek dam sahi approach chahiye — here's what toppers actually do differently for each subject.

📐

Quantitative Aptitude

Target: 18–22/25 marks | Most time-consuming subject — practice speed tricks!

  • Master tables (up to 30), squares (up to 30), cubes (up to 20) — saves 5–10 sec per question
  • Learn Vedic Math shortcuts for multiplication and percentage calculations
  • For Geometry and Trigonometry, memorise key formulas and standard values (sin 30°, cos 45° etc.)
  • Solve minimum 30 DI questions per week — it's an easy 3–5 marks if fast
  • Don't attempt Algebra questions that take more than 90 seconds — skip and revisit
  • Practice Profit & Loss, Time & Work with ratio method — fastest approach
📚 Best books: R.S. Aggarwal (Quantitative Aptitude), Rakesh Yadav Class Notes
🔤

English Language

Target: 20–23/25 marks | Highest scoring section for consistent readers

  • Read The Hindu or Indian Express editorial daily — improves vocab and reading speed together
  • Learn 10 new words daily with meaning, synonym, antonym, and usage in a sentence
  • For Error Spotting: revise Subject-Verb Agreement, Pronoun errors, Tense errors — these repeat every year
  • Cloze Test = reading comprehension lite. Read the full passage first, then fill blanks by context
  • Para Jumbles: identify the opening sentence (no pronoun reference without noun), then build logically
  • One-word substitution — a new list of ~200 common words is worth memorising completely
📚 Best books: Plinth to Paramount (K.D. Campus), S.P. Bakshi (Arihant English)
🌍

General Knowledge

Target: 16–20/25 marks | Pure memory game — but very scorable with smart prep

  • Static GK first: focus on Polity (articles, amendments), History (freedom movement), Geography (rivers, peaks, states)
  • For current affairs: follow last 6 months before exam date. Stick to one monthly PDF magazine (Drishti, Pratiyogita Darpan)
  • Science GK: cover NCERT Class 9–10 Science — most biology and physics questions come from here
  • Awards & Honours: keep a running note of Nobel Prizes, Bharat Ratna, Padma awards each year
  • Economy: understand basics — GDP, inflation, RBI policies, Union Budget highlights
  • Revise GK in short bursts of 20 minutes daily — spaced repetition is more effective than marathon sessions
📚 Best books: Lucent's GK, NCERT Class 9–12, Monthly current affairs PDF
🧩

General Intelligence & Reasoning

Target: 22–25/25 marks | Easiest section to score full marks with practice

  • Series questions: train yourself to identify arithmetic, geometric, and alphabetical patterns within 20 seconds
  • Analogy: practice 50–70 questions daily — the pattern types repeat every year
  • Coding-Decoding: letter position mapping (+/-), opposite letter tricks (A↔Z, B↔Y) — learn all 4 standard types
  • Mirror Image / Paper Folding: visual practice daily. Draw out and visualise — don't just read the question
  • Puzzles & Seating Arrangement: attempt all of these — they're logical and rarely require memorisation
  • Blood Relations: use a family tree diagram approach — never attempt these mentally without drawing
📚 Best resource: Previous year SSC CGL papers (2017–2024) — 80% pattern repeats!

Free Sample Questions

10 SSC CGL Practice Questions with Explanations

Real-exam style questions — attempt them before revealing the answer. GK + Reasoning + Quant + English covered.

Q1
🌍 General Knowledge — Indian Polity
Which Article of the Indian Constitution abolished untouchability?
  • A) Article 14
  • B) Article 17 ✓
  • C) Article 21
  • D) Article 32
Explanation: Article 17 of the Indian Constitution abolishes "untouchability" and forbids its practice in any form. Enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability is treated as an offence. Article 14 = Equality before law; Article 21 = Right to Life; Article 32 = Right to Constitutional Remedies.
Q2
🧩 General Intelligence — Number Series
Find the missing number: 3, 7, 13, 21, 31, ?
  • A) 41
  • B) 43
  • C) 43 ✓
  • D) 45
Explanation: The differences between consecutive terms are: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 (increasing by 2 each time). So the next term = 31 + 12 = 43. Always look at the difference pattern first in number series — it solves ~60% of series questions instantly.
Q3
🌍 General Knowledge — History
The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in which year?
  • A) 1940
  • B) 1941
  • C) 1942 ✓
  • D) 1945
Explanation: The Quit India Movement (also called the August Kranti or Bharat Chhodo Andolan) was launched on 8 August 1942 at the Bombay session of the All India Congress Committee. Gandhi gave the slogan "Do or Die" (करो या मरो). This is a frequently asked SSC CGL GK question.
Q4
🧩 General Intelligence — Analogy
Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?
  • A) Book
  • B) Student
  • C) School ✓
  • D) Knowledge
Explanation: The relationship is "Person : Place of work". A Doctor works in a Hospital; a Teacher works in a School. Always identify the type of relationship (object:use, person:tool, person:place, etc.) before jumping to the answer — this eliminates wrong options fast.
Q5
🌍 General Knowledge — Geography
Which is the longest river entirely within India?
  • A) Ganga
  • B) Godavari ✓
  • C) Yamuna
  • D) Krishna
Explanation: Godavari (≈1,465 km) is the longest river that lies entirely within India. The Ganga (2,525 km) is longer but originates in India and flows into Bangladesh. Godavari is also called "Dakshin Ganga" (Ganga of the South) — a common SSC trick question!
Q6
🧩 General Intelligence — Coding–Decoding
In a certain code language, MANGO is written as OCPIQ. How will APPLE be written in the same code?
  • A) CRRNG ✓
  • B) BQQMF
  • C) CRRNF
  • D) DSSOG
Explanation: Each letter is shifted forward by +2 positions. M→O, A→C, N→P, G→I, O→Q. So: A→C, P→R, P→R, L→N, E→G = CRRNG. When you see a coding pattern, immediately check +1, +2, –1, –2 shifts. Most SSC coding questions use simple shift patterns.
Q7
🌍 General Knowledge — Science
Which gas is responsible for the green colour in plants (chlorophyll)?
  • A) Nitrogen
  • B) Magnesium ✓
  • C) Iron
  • D) Calcium
Explanation: Magnesium (Mg) is the central atom in the chlorophyll molecule — it gives chlorophyll its green colour. This is a common SSC biology trick question. Note: Iron (Fe) deficiency causes yellowing of leaves (chlorosis), and Nitrogen deficiency slows growth — but the green colour element in chlorophyll is Magnesium.
Q8
🧩 General Intelligence — Blood Relations
Pointing to a woman in a photograph, a man said, "She is the sister of the father of my mother's son." How is the woman related to the man?
  • A) Grandmother
  • B) Aunt (Bua) ✓
  • C) Mother
  • D) Sister
Explanation: "My mother's son" = the man himself. "Father of my mother's son" = the man's own father. "Sister of the father" = the man's father's sister = the man's Aunt (Bua/Phuphi). Always draw a family tree for blood relation questions — never solve them in your head!
Q9
🌍 General Knowledge — Economy
Which organisation publishes the Human Development Index (HDI) every year?
  • A) World Bank
  • B) IMF
  • C) UNDP ✓
  • D) WHO
Explanation: The Human Development Index (HDI) is published annually by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in its Human Development Report. India's HDI rank in 2023 was 134 out of 193 countries. Trick: "Development" in both HDI and UNDP — helps remember the association!
Q10
🧩 General Intelligence — Direction Sense
Ravi starts from his house, walks 5 km North, turns Right and walks 3 km, then turns Right again and walks 5 km. How far is he from his starting point?
  • A) 1 km
  • B) 2 km
  • C) 3 km ✓
  • D) 5 km
Explanation: Start (S) → 5 km North (A) → 3 km East (B) → 5 km South (C). Since he went 5N then 5S, his vertical displacement = 0. He is now 3 km East of starting point. Distance = 3 km. Draw direction diagrams quickly for these — they're easy 1-mark questions!

Avoid These Mistakes

8 Common Mistakes SSC CGL Aspirants Make

These mistakes cost lakhs of candidates their dream every year. Learn from them so you don't repeat them, yaar.

❌

Skipping Negative Marking Strategy

Attempting all 100 questions blindly. Random guessing with 0.5 –ve marking can cost you 5–10 marks.

Attempt only if you can eliminate 2 options. Otherwise, skip. Attempt 80–90 with 80%+ accuracy beats 100 with 65% accuracy.
❌

Ignoring English Completely

Many Hindi-medium students avoid English and lose 15–20 easy marks. English is the most consistent scoring section.

Spend just 45 mins daily on English. Cloze test, error spotting, vocab — these don't require fluency, just pattern recognition.
❌

Studying New Topics Before Exam

Starting a brand-new topic in the last week creates confusion and reduces confidence for topics already prepared.

Last 10 days = revision only. Revise your notes, do mocks, sleep well. No new topics after Day 20.
❌

Not Taking Timed Mock Tests

Solving questions one-by-one without exam simulation. Many toppers say practice without timing is nearly useless for SSC.

Take at least 10 full timed mocks before exam. Practice attempting 100 Qs in exactly 60 minutes, under exam conditions.
❌

Relying Only on One Book

Using only R.S. Aggarwal for Quant or Lucent for GK and ignoring previous year papers is a huge mistake.

Previous Year Papers (2018–2024) are the single best resource. SSC repeats patterns and sometimes exact questions across shifts.
❌

Poor Current Affairs Preparation

Ignoring current affairs or covering too broad a period. Many students read 12 months of news — SSC focuses on last 6 months.

Follow last 6 months current affairs only. One good monthly PDF (Pratiyogita Darpan or GKToday) is enough — don't collect 10 sources.
❌

Not Analysing Mock Test Results

Taking 20 mocks and never properly reviewing wrong answers — you keep making the same mistakes.

For every 60-min mock, spend 60 mins analysing. Understand why you got each wrong answer wrong. Maintain an error log.
❌

Underestimating Reasoning Section

Thinking Reasoning is "auto" and not practising it enough. Many candidates score only 17–18 when 23–25 is easily achievable.

With 2 weeks of focused Reasoning practice (30 Qs daily), you can reliably score 23+/25. It's the highest ROI subject in SSC CGL.

SSC CGL 2025 — Common Doubts Answered

Aspirants ke sabse common questions — sab ka jawab yahan hai.

What is the eligibility criteria for SSC CGL 2025? +

Educational Qualification: Bachelor's degree from a recognised university in any discipline. Final year students can also apply (subject to producing the degree before joining).

Age Limit: 18–32 years for most posts. Relaxations: SC/ST candidates get 5 years, OBC get 3 years, PwD candidates get 10 years.

Nationality: Must be an Indian citizen. Subjects of Nepal and Bhutan, Tibetan refugees (before 1962), and persons of Indian origin who migrated from specific countries may also be eligible under special conditions.

How many attempts are allowed in SSC CGL? +

SSC CGL has no fixed attempt limit. You can appear as many times as you want as long as you fall within the eligible age range for the specific post you are applying for. The age limit varies: 18–27 years for some posts, 18–30 for others, and 20–32 for certain posts like Assistant Audit Officer.

This means as long as you are within the age bracket, apply every year until you crack it!

How many vacancies are expected in SSC CGL 2025? +

The SSC CGL 2024-25 notification is expected to announce approximately 17,000–18,000 vacancies across Group B (Gazetted/Non-Gazetted) and Group C posts. These include roles like:

  • Inspector (Income Tax, CBI, Customs, Excise) — highly sought-after
  • Assistant Section Officer — MEA, CSS, CVC, AFHQ
  • Sub-Inspector (CBI, NIA)
  • Junior Statistical Officer (JSO)
  • Assistant Audit Officer / Accounts Officer
  • Tax Assistant, Divisional Accountant, UDC
What is the salary after clearing SSC CGL? +

SSC CGL salary depends on the post and pay level:

  • Pay Level 8 (₹47,600–₹1,51,100): Assistant Audit Officer, Assistant Accounts Officer
  • Pay Level 7 (₹44,900–₹1,42,400): Junior Statistical Officer, Assistant Section Officer
  • Pay Level 6 (₹35,400–₹1,12,400): Inspector (IT, Customs, CBI), Sub-Inspector CBI
  • Pay Level 4 (₹25,500–₹81,100): Tax Assistant, UDC, Court Clerk

With HRA, DA, and other allowances, in-hand salary ranges from ₹35,000 to ₹90,000+ per month depending on posting city.

Is there negative marking in SSC CGL Tier 1? +

Yes. Tier 1 has a negative marking of 0.5 marks for each incorrect answer. Tier 2 Paper I has a negative marking of 1 mark per wrong answer.

Strategy: Only attempt if you can eliminate at least 2 wrong options. If you're completely clueless, skip. Random guessing on 20 questions with 0.5 –ve marking = –5 marks, which can drop your rank by thousands.

What is the SSC CGL Tier 1 cut-off typically? +

The cut-off varies by category and year. Approximate Tier 1 cut-offs from recent cycles:

  • General/UR: 140–155 out of 200
  • OBC: 130–145 out of 200
  • SC: 120–135 out of 200
  • ST: 110–125 out of 200

Target: Score at least 160+ for General category to be safe for Tier 2 shortlisting, as post preferences further filter candidates.

Can I prepare for SSC CGL while working a job? +

Absolutely — many SSC CGL qualifiers have cracked it while working full-time. The key is consistent 3–4 hours of focused daily study rather than marathon 10-hour weekend sessions. Here's a working professional routine:

  • Morning (30–45 min): Current affairs + 10 vocab words
  • Commute (30 min): Mobile MCQ practice (CareerAI works great here!)
  • Evening (2–2.5 hrs): Core subject study (Quant + Reasoning)
  • Weekend: Full mock test + review

With this schedule consistently for 3–4 months, clearing Tier 1 is very achievable.
